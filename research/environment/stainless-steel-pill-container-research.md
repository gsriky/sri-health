## 3. Why Stainless Steel Over Plastic?

### Health Concerns with Plastic Pill Containers

1. **BPA and Endocrine Disruptors**
   - BPA and similar chemicals may mimic hormones and disrupt endocrine functions
   - Even "BPA-free" plastics may contain other harmful chemicals (BPS, BPF) that are not necessarily safer
   - Phthalates in softer plastics are associated with developmental and reproductive concerns

2. **Microplastics**
   - Plastic containers shed microplastic particles, especially with repeated opening/closing, washing, and temperature changes
   - People are exposed to microplastics from plastic food and pill containers
   - Heat and UV light accelerate plastic degradation and chemical leaching

3. **Chemical Leaching**
   - Heat can cause plastic to degrade, potentially leaching chemicals into pills
   - This is particularly concerning for supplements stored in warm environments (cars, bathrooms, kitchens)
   - Supplements sitting in plastic containers for weeks/months have prolonged contact time

### Advantages of Stainless Steel

1. **Chemical Inertness:** Does not leach BPA, phthalates, or other chemicals
2. **No Microplastics:** Zero microplastic exposure
3. **Durability:** Virtually indestructible, does not crack, break, or degrade over time
4. **Easy to Clean:** Dishwasher safe, no bacterial harbor in cracks
5. **Temperature Resistant:** Will not warp or degrade in heat
6. **Sustainability:** Lasts a lifetime, fully recyclable
7. **Food-Grade Safety:** 304 stainless steel is widely recognized as safe for food contact

### Why This Matters for Biohackers

Given Bryan Johnson's focus on eliminating environmental toxins and microplastics (and the broader longevity community's concern about endocrine disruptors), using stainless steel for supplement storage aligns with the principle of minimizing unnecessary chemical exposures. If you are taking 10-40+ supplements daily, reducing plastic contact with those supplements is a logical step.

## 5. Concerns About Storing Supplements in Metal Containers

### Moisture

- **Primary concern:** Moisture is the biggest enemy of supplement stability
- Water-soluble vitamins (Vitamin C, B vitamins) are especially susceptible to moisture-induced degradation
- **Deliquescence** can occur at humidity as low as 30% -- crystalline supplements dissolve and lose potency
- Once degradation occurs from moisture, it is **irreversible** even if conditions improve
- **Mitigation:** Use containers with silicone O-ring seals or tight-fitting lids; add small silica gel packets if needed

### Metal Reactivity

- **Stainless steel (304/316 grade):** Extremely low reactivity. Chromium oxide layer prevents interaction with contents. This is the safest metal option.
- **Aluminum (anodized):** The anodized coating creates a protective barrier. Generally safe for short-term storage (1-2 weeks). Ikigai and mbarc use anodized aluminum with food-safe coatings.
- **Tin:** Uncoated tin can leach into contents over time. Avoid uncoated tin for long-term storage.
- **Specific vitamin interactions:** Vitamin C (ascorbic acid) is mildly acidic and theoretically could interact with uncoated metals over extended periods. In practice, pills/capsules have their own coatings that provide a barrier.

### Light Protection

- Metal containers provide **excellent light protection** (opaque), which is beneficial for light-sensitive supplements (Vitamin B2, Vitamin A, probiotics, fish oil)
- This is actually an advantage over glass or clear plastic containers

### Temperature

- Metal conducts heat more readily than plastic, so supplements in metal containers in hot environments (e.g., a car) may experience slightly elevated temperatures
- **Mitigation:** Do not leave metal pill cases in direct sunlight or hot cars

### Best Practices for Metal Container Storage

1. **Keep containers sealed** when not in use
2. **Do not store in bathrooms** (high humidity)
3. **Pre-load only 1-2 weeks at a time** -- do not leave supplements in secondary containers for months
4. **Ensure hands are dry** when handling supplements
5. **Choose containers with O-ring or silicone seals** for moisture protection
6. **Consider adding a small silica gel packet** inside larger containers
7. **Use food-grade stainless steel (304 or 316)** or anodized aluminum -- avoid raw/uncoated metals
