# Andrew Huberman Protocols - Detailed Reference

This document contains detailed breakdowns of key Huberman Lab protocols.

## Sleep Protocol (Complete)

### Morning Light Exposure
**Goal**: Set circadian rhythm, optimize cortisol timing, improve nighttime sleep

**Protocol**:
1. View natural sunlight within 30-60 minutes of waking
2. Duration: 10 minutes on clear days, 20-30 minutes on cloudy days
3. No sunglasses (prescription glasses OK)
4. Even on overcast days, outdoor light >> indoor light
5. Looking toward sun (not directly at it) - horizon viewing ideal

**Science**: Light through eyes activates melanopsin ganglion cells → signals suprachiasmatic nucleus → sets master circadian clock → triggers morning cortisol pulse → programs evening melatonin release

### Caffeine Timing
**Goal**: Avoid afternoon crash, preserve adenosine clearing

**Protocol**:
1. Delay caffeine 90-120 minutes after waking
2. This allows adenosine to clear naturally first
3. Stop caffeine intake 8-10 hours before bed
4. Exception: If exercising early, can have caffeine before workout

**Science**: Adenosine builds during wakefulness, creating sleep pressure. Caffeine blocks adenosine receptors but doesn't clear adenosine. If consumed immediately upon waking, adenosine continues building behind the "caffeine wall," causing afternoon crash when caffeine wears off.

### Temperature Manipulation
**Goal**: Facilitate sleep onset and maintenance

**Protocol**:
1. Keep bedroom cool (65-68°F / 18-20°C)
2. Hot bath/shower 1-2 hours before bed (paradoxically cools core)
3. Allow hands and feet to release heat (no heavy socks)
4. If wake at night, expose palms/soles to cool air

**Science**: Core body temperature must drop 1-3°F for sleep onset. Warming extremities causes vasodilation → heat loss from core. Hot bath accelerates this by first raising then dropping temperature.

### Evening Light Avoidance
**Goal**: Protect melatonin production, maintain dopamine

**Protocol**:
1. Dim lights after sunset
2. Avoid bright overhead lights (especially 10pm-4am)
3. Use low-positioned warm lights (candles, dim lamps)
4. Blue light filters helpful but not sufficient alone
5. If must use screens, keep at lowest brightness

**Science**: Light at night (especially 11pm-4am) suppresses dopamine release and disrupts circadian rhythm. Low-angle light (like sunset) is interpreted differently than overhead light by the brain.

### Sleep Supplements Stack
**Goal**: Enhance sleep onset and quality without dependency

**Core Stack (30-60 min before bed)**:
| Supplement | Dose | Mechanism |
|------------|------|-----------|
| Magnesium L-threonate | 140mg (of elemental Mg) | Crosses blood-brain barrier, activates GABA |
| Apigenin | 50mg | Binds chloride channels, reduces anxiety |
| L-theanine | 100-300mg | Increases GABA, reduces excitatory neurotransmission |

**Optional Additions (cycle, not nightly)**:
| Supplement | Dose | When to Use |
|------------|------|-------------|
| Myo-inositol | 900mg | Helps with middle-of-night waking |
| Glycine | 2g | Every 3-4 nights for deeper sleep |
| GABA | 100mg | Occasional use for anxiety |

**Important**: Don't take glycine, GABA, and myo-inositol together every night - cycle them.

## NSDR (Non-Sleep Deep Rest) Protocol

### What is NSDR?
A term coined by Huberman encompassing practices that induce deep relaxation without sleep, including Yoga Nidra, hypnosis, and specific relaxation techniques.

### Benefits (Research-Backed)
- 65% increase in baseline dopamine after 1-hour session (Copenhagen study)
- Improved attention, working memory, recognition memory (Dr. Wendy Suzuki)
- Reduced anxiety in response to psychosocial stress
- Can partially compensate for lost sleep

### Protocol Options

**Quick Reset (10 minutes)**:
- Use for: dopamine restoration, focus reset, midday energy
- Method: Guided NSDR audio (hubermanlab.com/nsdr)
- Timing: After lunch, before demanding cognitive work

**Sleep Debt Recovery (30-60 minutes)**:
- Use for: recovering from poor night's sleep
- Method: Yoga Nidra or long-form NSDR
- Timing: Early afternoon (not too close to bedtime)

**Learning Enhancement**:
- Use for: accelerating skill acquisition
- Method: 20-min NSDR after learning session
- Timing: Within 1 hour of learning new material

### How to Practice
1. Lie down in comfortable position
2. Close eyes
3. Follow audio guidance (body scan, intention setting)
4. Remain still but awake
5. Allow thoughts to pass without engagement
6. Complete the full session (don't cut short)

### Recommended Resources
- Huberman Lab NSDR scripts: hubermanlab.com/nsdr
- Yoga Nidra apps: Insight Timer, Yoga Nidra Network
- Huberman-approved: Virtusan app NSDR sessions

## Focus & Productivity Protocol

### Ultradian Rhythm Work Blocks
**Goal**: Align work with natural focus cycles

**Protocol**:
1. Work in 90-minute focused blocks
2. First 5-10 minutes may feel unfocused (normal)
3. Peak focus typically 20-40 minutes in
4. Natural drop-off around 90 minutes
5. Take genuine break (10-20 min) between blocks
6. Maximum 2-3 deep work blocks per day

### Visual Focus for Mental Focus
**Goal**: Use visual system to engage prefrontal cortex

**Protocol**:
1. Focus eyes on single point (screen, paper, object)
2. Narrow visual attention (avoid looking around)
3. Blink rate naturally decreases
4. This engages frontal cortex focus networks
5. Take "panoramic vision" breaks to reduce stress

**Science**: Neural circuits for visual focus overlap with cognitive focus circuits. Narrowing visual attention reflexively engages the same circuits used for mental concentration.

### Dopamine Management
**Goal**: Maintain healthy baseline dopamine, avoid depletion

**Principles**:
1. Don't layer too many dopamine-releasing activities
2. Cold exposure: spike + recovery strengthens baseline
3. Random intermittent reward > constant reward
4. Avoid substances that cause massive spikes (crash follows)
5. Celebration after effort, not anticipation before

**For Deadline Focus**:
- L-tyrosine: 500mg (amino acid precursor to dopamine)
- Take on empty stomach, morning
- Don't use daily (reserve for key sessions)

### Cold Exposure for Focus
**Goal**: Sustainable dopamine increase (2.5x lasting hours)

**Protocol**:
1. Cold shower or ice bath
2. Temperature: uncomfortably cold but safe
3. Duration: 1-3 minutes (less is often enough)
4. End on cold (not warm)
5. Best timing: morning or early afternoon
6. Frequency: 3-4x per week

**Science**: Cold triggers norepinephrine (2-3x) and dopamine (2.5x) release. Unlike stimulants, the dopamine increase is gradual and long-lasting (hours). This builds stress resilience over time.

## Stress & Anxiety Protocol

### Physiological Sigh (Real-Time Stress Intervention)
**Goal**: Rapidly reduce stress in the moment

**Protocol**:
1. Double inhale through nose (full inhale + quick top-off)
2. Long, slow exhale through mouth
3. 1-3 cycles typically sufficient
4. Can be done covertly (meetings, conversations)

**Science**: Double inhale reinflates collapsed alveoli in lungs. Long exhale activates parasympathetic nervous system. This is the fastest known behavioral method for reducing autonomic arousal.

### Deliberate Cold Exposure for Stress Resilience
**Goal**: Build capacity to handle stress (not just immediate relief)

**Protocol**:
1. Regular cold exposure (see Focus protocol)
2. Practice "walls" of stress resistance mentally
3. Focus on controlling breath despite cold
4. This translates to better stress handling in daily life

**Science**: Cold creates real physiological stress response. Learning to manage this builds neural circuits for stress regulation that transfer to other contexts.

### Panoramic Vision (Optic Flow)
**Goal**: Reduce amygdala activation, calm nervous system

**Protocol**:
1. Relax gaze to take in wide field of view
2. Don't focus on any single point
3. Include peripheral vision
4. Walking outdoors with forward optic flow ideal
5. 5-10 minutes can significantly reduce stress

**Science**: Focused vision activates alertness circuits. Panoramic vision is associated with relaxed states. Optic flow (things moving past you) has additional calming effect.

## Heat & Cold Exposure Protocol

### Deliberate Cold Exposure
**Goal**: Dopamine, metabolism, stress resilience, inflammation reduction

**Protocol**:
- Temperature: Cold enough to want to get out but safe
- Duration: 11 minutes total per week (can be split)
- Method: Cold shower, ice bath, cold plunge
- Timing: Morning/afternoon (not close to bedtime)
- End on cold (not warm) for maximum benefit

### Deliberate Heat Exposure (Sauna)
**Goal**: Growth hormone, cardiovascular health, heat shock proteins

**Protocol**:
- Temperature: 176-212°F (80-100°C)
- Duration: 20 minutes, 2-3x per week
- Hydrate well before and after
- Can combine with cold (heat first, then cold)

### Combining Heat and Cold
**Sequence**: Heat → Cold (if doing same session)
**Reasoning**: Cold at end preserves the metabolic/dopamine benefits

## Learning & Memory Protocol

### Sleep-Dependent Memory Consolidation
1. Learn new material during focused waking states
2. Sleep (or NSDR) within 24 hours of learning
3. First night's sleep especially critical
4. REM sleep consolidates emotional/procedural memory
5. Deep sleep consolidates declarative memory

### Accelerated Learning Protocol
1. High-focus learning session (90 min max)
2. NSDR (10-20 min) immediately or soon after
3. Quality sleep that night
4. Space repetitions over days (not cramming)
5. Test yourself rather than passive review

### Neuroplasticity Triggers
1. High focus/attention (creates neurochemical state)
2. Urgency/importance (epinephrine release)
3. Repetition within session
4. Sleep for consolidation
5. NSDR can substitute partially for sleep

## Nutrition Protocol

### Core Principles
1. **Behavioral foundation**: Meal timing affects circadian rhythm
2. **Protein priority**: Critical for muscle preservation, especially with age
3. **Individualized approach**: No single diet works for everyone
4. **Supplements support, not replace**: Food first

### Meal Timing
**Goal**: Align eating with circadian biology

**Protocol**:
1. First meal: 1-2 hours after waking (not immediately)
2. Last meal: 3+ hours before bed
3. Most calories earlier in day when possible
4. Consistent meal timing day-to-day

**Science**: Eating is a zeitgeber (time-giver) that helps set circadian rhythm. Late eating disrupts sleep architecture and glucose regulation.

### Protein Guidelines
**Goal**: Preserve muscle mass, support recovery

**Protocol**:
- **Minimum**: 0.7g per pound body weight daily
- **Optimal**: 1g per pound of lean body mass
- **Distribution**: Spread across meals (not all at once)
- **Quality**: Complete proteins with all essential amino acids

### Blood Glucose Management
**Goal**: Stable energy, metabolic health

**Protocol**:
1. Eat fiber/vegetables before carbohydrates
2. Add protein and fat to carb-heavy meals
3. Walk after meals (even 10-15 minutes helps)
4. Consider CGM for personalized insights

## Light Protocol (Comprehensive)

### Morning Light Protocol
**Goal**: Set circadian rhythm, optimize cortisol, improve nighttime sleep

**Protocol**:
1. View sunlight within 30-60 minutes of waking
2. Duration: 10 min (sunny), 20-30 min (cloudy)
3. No sunglasses; prescription glasses OK
4. Don't stare at sun—look toward horizon
5. Even overcast sky >> indoor lighting

### Daytime Light
**Goal**: Maintain alertness, support mood

**Protocol**:
1. Get outdoor light exposure throughout day when possible
2. Work near windows
3. Use bright artificial light if no natural light access
4. Aim for 10,000+ lux during work hours

### Evening Light Protocol
**Goal**: Protect melatonin, support sleep onset

**Protocol**:
1. Dim lights 2-3 hours before bed
2. Use low-positioned lights (floor lamps, candles)
3. Avoid overhead lights after sunset
4. Blue blockers helpful but not sufficient alone

### Critical Window: 11pm-4am
**Goal**: Protect dopamine and circadian system

**Protocol**:
1. Minimize ALL light exposure during this window
2. If must have light, use very dim red/orange
3. This window most sensitive to light damage
4. Even brief bright light exposure is harmful

### Seasonal Considerations
**Protocol for Winter/Low Light**:
1. Light therapy lamp: 10,000 lux, 20-30 min morning
2. Position at eye level or slightly above
3. Use within first hour of waking
4. SAD lamp can substitute for sunlight

## Creativity Protocol

### The Creativity Framework
Huberman distinguishes between two types of creative thinking:
- **Divergent thinking**: Generating novel ideas, brainstorming
- **Convergent thinking**: Narrowing down, implementing solutions

### Divergent Thinking Protocol
**Goal**: Generate novel ideas and possibilities

**Protocol**:
1. **Relaxed alertness**: Not too focused, not too drowsy
2. **Panoramic vision**: Soft gaze, wide visual field
3. **Movement**: Walking (especially outdoors) enhances ideation
4. **NSDR beforehand**: Primes creative networks
5. **No judgment phase**: Capture all ideas without filtering

**Best times**: Early morning (before full alertness) or during NSDR transition states

### Convergent Thinking Protocol
**Goal**: Refine and implement ideas

**Protocol**:
1. **Focused attention**: Narrow visual focus
2. **High alertness**: Caffeine/stimulation appropriate
3. **Time pressure**: Moderate urgency helps
4. **Single-task**: No distractions

**Best times**: Mid-morning to early afternoon (peak alertness)

### Sleep & Creativity
**Protocol**:
1. Problem exposure before sleep
2. Don't try to solve—just expose mind to problem
3. Sleep (especially REM) processes creatively
4. Capture ideas immediately upon waking

### NSDR for Creative Insight
**Protocol**:
1. Brief NSDR (10-20 min) after intensive creative work
2. Allows subconscious processing
3. Often insights emerge during or after NSDR
4. Keep capture tool nearby

**Goal**: Strengthen bonding neurochemistry

## Oral, Gut & Microbiome Protocol

### Oral Microbiome Protocol
**Goal**: Support oral health without disrupting beneficial bacteria

**Protocol**:
1. **Avoid antiseptic mouthwashes**: Kill good bacteria, reduce nitric oxide
2. **Tongue scraping**: Morning, before eating
3. **Xylitol**: May support oral health
4. **Nasal breathing**: Promotes oral health vs mouth breathing
5. **Regular dental care**: Professional cleanings

**Why it matters**: Oral microbiome produces nitric oxide (cardiovascular health) and communicates with gut microbiome.

### Gut Microbiome Protocol
**Goal**: Support diverse, healthy gut bacteria

**Protocol**:
1. **Fermented foods**: 2-4 servings daily
   - Sauerkraut (unpasteurized)
   - Kimchi
   - Yogurt/kefir (live cultures)
   - Kombucha
2. **Fiber diversity**: Many different plant sources
3. **Prebiotic foods**: Feed beneficial bacteria
   - Garlic, onion, leeks
   - Asparagus, bananas
   - Oats, apples

**Stanford Research**: 4 servings of fermented foods daily improved inflammatory markers more than high-fiber diet alone.

### Gut Support Supplements
| Supplement | Dose | Purpose |
|------------|------|---------|
| L-Glutamine | 1g daily | Gut lining support |
| Ginger | With meals | Digestive support |
| Garlic | 600mg with meals | TMAO offset, microbiome support |

### Gut-Brain Axis
**Understanding**: Bidirectional communication between gut and brain via vagus nerve, immune signals, and microbial metabolites.

**Protocol for Gut-Brain Health**:
1. Stress management (stress harms microbiome)
2. Sleep optimization (sleep affects gut)
3. Diverse diet (supports diverse microbiome)
4. Limit processed foods and artificial sweeteners
5. Consider gut health when mood/cognition issues arise

### What to Avoid
- Unnecessary antibiotics (disrupt microbiome)
- Antiseptic mouthwash (kills oral microbiome)
- Excessive alcohol
- Artificial sweeteners (may disrupt microbiome)
- Chronic stress (harms gut lining and bacteria)

## Motivation Protocol

### Dopamine Baseline Management
**Goal**: Maintain healthy baseline dopamine for sustained motivation

**Principles**:
1. **Avoid stacking**: Don't layer multiple dopamine-releasing activities
2. **Intermittent reward**: Random rewards > constant rewards
3. **Effort before reward**: Celebrate completion, not anticipation
4. **Protect baseline**: Large spikes lead to crashes

### Protocol for Sustained Motivation
**Goal**: Maintain drive without burnout

**Protocol**:
1. **Morning**: Don't check phone immediately—delay gratification
2. **Work sessions**: Single focus, no background entertainment
3. **Rewards**: After work completion, not during
4. **Cold exposure**: Builds sustainable dopamine (2.5x, lasting hours)
5. **NSDR**: Restores dopamine without spike/crash

### Overcoming Procrastination
**Protocol**:
1. Start with smallest possible action (2 min)
2. Use visual focus to engage attention circuits
3. Set arbitrary time constraint (creates urgency)
4. Remove friction from starting
5. Dopamine follows action, not motivation

### Avoiding Dopamine Depletion
**Warning signs**:
- Needing more stimulation for same effect
- Difficulty enjoying simple pleasures
- Chronic low motivation
- Seeking constant novelty

**Recovery protocol**:
1. Reduce high-dopamine activities for 24-48 hours
2. Embrace "boredom"
3. NSDR to restore baseline
4. Gradual reintroduction of rewards
